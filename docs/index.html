Democratizing Aviation Emissions Estimation

An open-source, data-driven methodology for system-wide U.S. aviation emissions accounting — integrating BTS, FAA, ICAO, and EUROCONTROL data to estimate per-flight CO₂, CO₂e, NOx, CO, and HC at the granularity of the specific aircraft and engine flown.

Data Sources
Built on Four Public Data Providers

The framework joins flight-level, aircraft-level, and engine-level data to connect a specific flight to the specific engine that flew it — rather than relying on a representative aircraft or a network-wide average.

✈️
BTS On-Time
Flight Records
Monthly U.S. domestic flight schedules since 1987 — tail number, route, distance, taxi and air time for every flight.
🛩️
FAA Registry
Aircraft & Engine
Tail number → engine code + aircraft model, via the FAA's Master and Aircraft Reference tables. Cross-referenced with BTS Schedule B-43 for seat counts.
🌍
ICAO EEDB
LTO Emissions
Certified emission indices for 800+ engine models — take-off, climb-out, approach, and idle phases of the Landing/Take-Off (LTO) cycle.
📊
EUROCONTROL BADA
CCD Emissions
Distance-interpolated fuel and emissions data for the Cruise, Climb, and Descent (CCD) flight cycle, by aircraft type.
Methodology
Five-Stage Open-Source Pipeline

Results
Real Emissions Patterns, Reproduced from the Paper

A sample of what the full pipeline surfaces — all computed from this repo's own real output, not illustrative figures.

NOx > CO₂
Converted to CO₂-equivalent terms, NOx emissions exceed CO₂ alone in both the LTO and CCD flight cycles — the paper's headline finding, confirmed again on this repo's own computed data.
Frontier, Spirit lowest
Ranking airlines by CO₂ per seat-mile, Frontier and Spirit consistently rank lowest — a function of fleet and engine choice, not distance flown.
ATL, DFW, DEN highest
By local (LTO) emissions, Atlanta, Dallas/Fort Worth, and Denver are the highest-emitting U.S. airports — visible directly on the interactive route map.

About
Open-Source Research for U.S. Aviation

Developed by Andy G. Eskenazi, Landon G. Butler, Arnav P. Joshi, and Megan S. Ryerson at the University of Pennsylvania, this methodology was presented at the 10th International Conference on Research in Air Transportation (ICRAT 2022). It fills a gap in the emissions-estimation literature: existing tools are either system-wide but rely on simplifying heuristics, or granular but restricted to a single airport or region, or accurate but proprietary and inaccessible.

This repository is the actively maintained, open-source implementation — free for researchers, planners, and community advocates to run, extend, and audit.
